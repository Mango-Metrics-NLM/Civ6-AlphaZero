# Military Strategy Principles for Civilization VI

## Force Concentration and Mass

### The Principle of Mass
Concentrate combat power at decisive points to achieve overwhelming superiority. A force twice the size has exponentially greater combat effectiveness following Lanchester's Laws - a force with 3 units versus 2 has a 5-point advantage (3^2 - 2^2 = 5), not just 50%.

### Civ6 Application
- Concentrate military at a single decisive objective (enemy capital or strategic city)
- AI struggles with coordinated multi-front defense
- Surround target cities with overlapping zones of control
- Attack from multiple angles to maximize damage per turn
- "Mass" means concentrating effects - smaller superior forces can beat larger dispersed ones
- Timing attacks exploit this: concentrated Knights overwhelm scattered defenders

## Economy of Force

### Balanced Resource Allocation
Allocate minimum essential forces to secondary objectives while concentrating power at decisive points. Accept calculated risk on secondary fronts.

### Civ6 Application
- Early game: 2-3 warriors and archers deter barbarians and AI attacks
- Invest production in settlers, builders, and districts while maintaining minimal security
- During offensive operations: garrison minimum defense (one archer per city)
- Strip interior cities temporarily - AI cannot execute multi-front invasions
- Designate specialized military production centers with encampments
- Use "Raid" policy card to make attacking forces self-sustaining through pillaging

## Terrain and Defensive Advantage

### Terrain Combat Modifiers
- **Hills**: +3 combat strength when defending
- **Woods/Rainforest**: +3 combat strength when defending
- **Hills + Woods**: +6 combined combat strength
- **Marsh**: -2 penalty to defenders
- **River crossing**: +3 bonus to defender when attacker crosses

### Tactical Applications
- Position defending units on hills with forest coverage for +6 bonus
- Prioritize city locations surrounded by defensive terrain (hills, forests, mountains)
- Mountains provide adjacency bonuses and funnel enemies into kill zones
- Avoid attacking entrenched opponents on superior terrain
- Use ranged and siege units to soften defenders before committing melee
- Terrain bonuses can negate a full era technology advantage
- Rivers break zones of control for tactical repositioning

## Flanking and Envelopment

### Attacking from Multiple Directions
Concentrate offensive power where defenses are weakest - sides and rear.

### Civ6 Application
- Completely surround target cities to maximize damage and trigger "besieged" status
- Besieged status activates when all six adjacent tiles are within your zone of control
- Prevents wall repairs and stops health regeneration
- Position melee units for ZOC while ranged/siege bombard from second ring
- Use cavalry (which ignore ZOC) to bypass frontlines and strike vulnerable units
- Light and heavy cavalry exploit breakthroughs and pursue routing enemies

## Combined Arms Warfare

### Unit Type Synergy
Build balanced armies with complementary capabilities:

### Recommended Force Composition
- **Melee units**: Establish zone of control, absorb damage, capture cities
- **Ranged units**: Deal damage without retaliation, soften targets
- **Siege units**: Destroy city walls and defenses
- **Cavalry**: Rapid flanking, pillaging, exploiting breakthroughs
- **Support units**: Great Generals (+5 combat strength, +1 movement), Battering Rams, Siege Towers

### Timing Attack Tension
Tech research is linear and production limited - rushing one advanced unit type often proves more effective than diverse balanced armies. A force of Knights crushes mixed Spearmen/Swordsmen/Archers.

### Optimal Approach
Use combined arms within a technological tier: pair Knights with Crossbowmen, or Tanks with Infantry.

## Timing Attacks

### Exploiting Temporary Superiority
Timing attacks exploit windows when you've unlocked powerful units but opponents haven't developed counters.

### Key Timing Windows

1. **Heavy Chariot Upgrade Rush**
   - Build Heavy Chariots while researching The Wheel
   - Upgrade instantly when technology completes

2. **Swordsmen + Iron**
   - Prioritize Iron Working and secure iron
   - Overwhelm ancient-era defenses

3. **Crossbowmen (Machinery)**
   - 2 range and 40+ combat strength
   - Devastating against classical-era units

4. **Knights (Chivalry)** - STRONGEST TIMING
   - Rush directly to Chivalry
   - Pre-build Horsemen/Heavy Chariots for instant upgrade
   - Overwhelm opponents with Spearmen and Swordsmen

5. **Cavalry (Rifling)**
   - Major power spike
   - Long gap from Knights means opponents unprepared

### Key Insight
Having an era-superior unit matters more than having a counter-unit at the same era. Knights beat Spearmen more reliably than Pikemen beat Knights.

## Intelligence and Reconnaissance

### Information Advantage
Know enemy positions, strength, and intentions to enable decisive action.

### Civ6 Implementation
- Early scouts provide critical intelligence on terrain, city-states, rivals
- Maintain active scouting to track AI military buildups
- Diplomatic Visibility grants +3 combat strength per level difference
- Scout target thoroughly before declaring war:
  - Identify city defenses (walls, encampments)
  - Map terrain approaches
  - Assess garrison strength
- Plan invasion routes exploiting terrain advantages
- Civ6 provides exact combat odds - calculate optimal attack sequences
- Maintain vision of enemy territory even in peacetime

## Logistics and Strategic Resources

### Post-Gathering Storm Resource System
Strategic resources accumulate per turn. One improved source produces 2 units/turn, sustainably supporting 6 units (3:1 ratio).

### Strategic Resource Priority

1. **Iron** - Critical for Swordsmen, Legion, Samurai (classical backbone)
2. **Niter** - Required for Musketmen, Cavalry, Field Cannons (often scarce)
3. **Oil** - Powers modern armor and air forces
4. **Aluminum** - Required for jet fighters, bombers, space victory

### Resource Shortages
Lacking resources imposes severe penalties:
- Units cannot heal
- Combat strength penalties proportional to shortage
- Trade surplus resources with allies
- Use "Resource Management" policy to maximize efficiency

## Pillaging as Economic Warfare

### Pillage Yields by Improvement Type
- **Mines**: Gold + Science
- **Farms**: Gold + Food (heals pillaging unit)
- **Quarries/Pastures/Plantations/Camps**: Faith + Culture
- **Districts**: Massive gold + respective yield

### Strategic Applications

1. **Self-sustaining armies**
   - Generate gold to upgrade units
   - Maintain forces without taxing home production
   - Norway's Harald Hardrada exemplifies pillage economies

2. **Economic crippling**
   - Even without city capture, pillaging collapses enemy income
   - Weakens opponents for future invasions
   - Prevents alternative victory conditions

3. **Tactical healing**
   - Pillaging farms immediately heals damaged units
   - Sustain offensive operations without withdrawal

4. **Joint war exploitation**
   - Declare joint wars for gold per turn
   - Pillage extensively while ally provides diplomatic cover
   - "Raid" policy card increases pillage yields

### Advanced Strategy
Avoid capturing cities early in war - pillage everything for 10-20 turns to build gold reserves, then capture only the best cities.

## Zone of Control Mechanics

### ZOC Rules
- Melee units exert ZOC on all six adjacent tiles
- Ranged/siege units exert ZOC only on their own tile
- Cavalry units ignore ZOC entirely
- Units entering enemy ZOC must stop immediately
- Rivers break ZOC across water boundaries

### Tactical Applications

1. **Defensive screens**
   - Create overlapping ZOC networks
   - Channel enemy advances into kill zones
   - Ranged units behind screen fire repeatedly

2. **City siege**
   - Surround cities with six units establishing ZOC
   - Triggers "besieged" status preventing healing
   - Even ranged/siege contribute ZOC on their own tile

3. **Pinning forces**
   - Trap enemy units in unfavorable positions
   - Ranged units attack without retaliation
   - Units in ZOC cannot retreat except across rivers

4. **Cavalry exploitation**
   - Bypass enemy frontlines
   - Strike vulnerable siege, ranged, support units

## Deception and Strategic Surprise

### Sun Tzu's Principle
"When able to attack, we must seem unable; when using our forces, we must seem inactive."

### Civ6 Deception Tactics

1. **Peacetime positioning**
   - Keep main army inland or focused on another target
   - AI prepares defenses based on visible threats
   - Rapidly redeploy when declaring war

2. **Coastal raids**
   - Naval melee units strike from unexpected directions
   - Pillage coastal improvements and withdraw

3. **Secondary front exploitation**
   - While AI masses defenses on one border
   - Attack from unexpected direction
   - AI poorly manages multi-front wars

4. **Feigned peace**
   - Make peace treaties while rebuilding
   - Attack when opponent demobilizes
   - Exploit joint wars immediately after making peace

5. **Upgrade surprises**
   - Maintain obsolete units (Heavy Chariots, Horsemen)
   - Mass upgrade to Knights when declaring war
   - AI assesses threat on visible unit strength, not upgrade potential

## Campaign Phases

### Phase 1: Preparation (Before War Declaration)
- Scout target territory for city strength, defenses, terrain, resources
- Mass forces at decisive point (target capital or strategic city)
- Establish logistics: secure strategic resources, stockpile gold
- Position units for rapid offensive

### Phase 2: Offensive Operations
- Establish ZOC perimeter around target city for siege status
- Combined arms: siege destroys walls, ranged provides firepower, melee establishes ZOC and captures
- Pillage surrounding improvements for economic gain and healing
- Exploit terrain: attack from hills/forests, avoid attacking into defensive terrain

### Phase 3: Exploitation
- Use cavalry to pillage enemy infrastructure deeply
- Press attack while enemy is disorganized
- Don't allow recovery time
- Economy of force: minimum garrisons on captured cities

### Phase 4: Consolidation
- Repair critical improvements in captured cities
- Establish defensive perimeter using terrain advantages
- Rebuild forces for next offensive or transition to development
